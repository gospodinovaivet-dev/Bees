Welcome to the hive

BeeWorkers is a team of beekeepers, engineers and gardeners who look after honeybee colonies out in the countryside. We give bees safe homes, plant meadows full of food for them, and use smart, solar-powered hives to keep every colony healthy.

About us

About us

BeeWorkers started in 2019, when a few friends rescued a wild swarm from an old shed and gave it a proper home. Today we look after 38 colonies in three apiaries out in the countryside, far from busy roads and sprayed fields.

We're beekeepers, engineers and gardeners working side by side: the bees get safe homes and plenty of flowers, and the farms and wild places around them get busy pollinators.

  • 38 colonies
  • 3 apiaries
  • 12,000 m² of wildflowers planted
  • 1.2 t of honey shared last year

How we do it

We keep our hives where bees thrive: quiet countryside spots next to forests, meadows and orchards.

  • Health checks every week from spring to autumn
  • Solar-powered sensors that warn us about heat, cold or a hungry colony
  • We only take the extra honey, so the bees always keep enough for winter
  • New beekeepers learn from our team, hive by hive

Why we do it

Bees are in trouble, and without them a lot of the food and flowers we love would disappear.

  • Meadows are shrinking, so bees find less food
  • Pesticides and parasites like the varroa mite weaken colonies
  • A single healthy colony can hold tens of thousands of bees
  • Every hive we save helps the plants and animals around it

How we help the planet

Bees pollinate many of the fruits, vegetables and nuts we eat, and countless wild plants.

  • Our bees pollinate orchards and fields around every apiary
  • We plant native wildflowers along field edges and in clearings
  • Our hive sensors run on solar power
  • We visit schools to teach kids how to help bees at home

Our hives

All our apiaries are out in the countryside, beside forests, meadows and farms, where bees have clean air, fresh water and flowers all season long.

Forest Edge Apiary

📍 Sunny clearing by the forest

Our oldest apiary: hand-painted hives in a sheltered clearing. The trees block the wind, and the acacia and linden trees nearby give a light, flowery honey.

  • 12 colonies
  • Since 2019
  • Acacia & linden honey

Oak Wood Apiary

📍 Under the old oak trees

A quiet, shady spot where we train new beekeepers. Here we do our most careful health checks, frame by frame, and raise young queens for new colonies.

  • 8 colonies
  • Since 2021
  • Forest honey

Valley Fields Apiary

📍 Between orchards and wildflower fields

Our biggest apiary. In spring the bees pollinate the farmers' almond and fruit orchards, and every hive here has a solar-powered sensor.

  • 18 colonies
  • Since 2022
  • Wildflower honey
